Project overview

Digital First complements Verizon's ThingSpace IoT platform with a self-serve path: automate the steps that previously required a human to lower the barrier of entry, especially for smaller customers who don't warrant a dedicated sales motion. My work focused on the acquisition and onboarding experience.

The problem

Sales-assisted onboarding doesn't scale down well. Smaller IoT customers are expensive to serve manually and often fall through the cracks, while the complexity of the domain makes pure self-serve intimidating. The design problem was to automate onboarding without abandoning the customer in the hard parts — self-serve that still feels guided.

Users & research

Primary users are smaller and newer IoT customers who want to start without a sales call. I leaned on behavioral research and competitive analysis to find where self-serve typically breaks and how to keep automation from feeling like being left alone.

  • Customers want to start now, not wait for a rep.
  • The technical steps (connectivity, plans, provisioning) are where confidence collapses.
  • Automation has to carry the user through, not just remove the human.

Strategy & approach

Lower the barrier of entry by automating the mechanical steps and designing clear, confidence-building guidance around the technical ones. Treat cost-to-serve as a design lever: every step a customer can complete alone is a step that scales.

Design process

Self-serve acquisition and onboarding flows, automation touchpoints, and guidance patterns. Specific screens and flows are available on request.

Validation & iteration

For a self-serve onboarding product, I'd validate on completion without assistance, time-to-value, and where users still request help.

Outcome & impact — how I'd measure it

  • Self-serve onboarding completion rate (without sales assistance).
  • Cost-to-serve and deflection from assisted channels.
  • Time-to-value: sign-up to first working device.
  • Points where users still drop to human help.

What I'd do next · Reflections

Digital First was a lesson in designing automation that still feels human — the barrier you remove can't be replaced by abandonment. That same principle shapes how I think about agent-driven interfaces now: automate the mechanics, keep the person oriented.
