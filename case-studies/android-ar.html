Project overview

This was an Android initiative introducing a new augmented-reality product through interactive video. As visual designer I helped shape how the experience looked and how people moved through the interactive content that explained and demonstrated the AR capability.

The problem

New technology is easy to misunderstand. AR especially needs to be shown, not told. The design challenge was to use interactive video to make a novel capability feel obvious and desirable — communicating what AR could do without a wall of explanation.

Users & research

Primary audience is Android users encountering the AR product for the first time. I'm describing the communication problem I designed against: making a new AR capability instantly understandable.

Strategy & approach

Lead with demonstration. Use interactive video and strong visual design to let people experience the idea rather than read about it, keeping the interaction model simple enough that the novelty is in the AR, not in figuring out the interface.

Design process

Visual design and interaction direction for the interactive video experience. Specific frames and screens are available on request.

Validation & iteration

For interactive AR video, I'd validate on comprehension of the capability and engagement with the interactive elements.

Outcome & impact — how I'd measure it

  • Comprehension: do viewers understand what the AR product does afterward.
  • Interaction / completion rate on the interactive video.
  • Intent to try the AR feature.

What I'd do next · Reflections

Android AR sharpened my instinct for showing rather than telling — a skill that pays off whenever a product introduces something people haven't seen before, including today's agent-driven interfaces.
