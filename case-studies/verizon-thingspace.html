Project overview

ThingSpace is Verizon's IoT management platform. Customers connect devices and then need to see them, understand their status, and act on them. My work centered on the acquisition experiences that bring customers onto the platform, grounded in behavioral research and competitive analysis.

The problem

IoT adoption stalls when the path from "interested" to "first connected device" is complicated. Buyers have to understand connectivity, plans, and device management before they see any value. The design problem was to lower that barrier — make the value legible early and the acquisition path clear — while keeping the product honest about a genuinely technical domain.

Users & research

Primary users span small operators adding a few connected devices and enterprise teams managing large fleets. I used behavioral research and competitive analysis to understand where prospects hesitated and how competing IoT platforms framed the same decisions.

  • The jump from marketing promise to working device is where interest leaks out.
  • Fleet managers need monitoring and control at scale; newcomers need a gentle on-ramp.
  • Competitive framing shapes expectations before a customer ever signs up.

Strategy & approach

Design the acquisition experience to prove value before it asks for commitment, and to scale gracefully from one device to a fleet. Use competitive analysis to meet expectations prospects already carry, and behavioral research to remove the specific moments where they drop off.

Design process

Acquisition flows, portal patterns for track / monitor / control, and the connective tissue between them. I can share specific flows and screens on request.

Validation & iteration

For an IoT acquisition flow, I'd validate on activation (first device connected), comprehension of plans, and drop-off between steps.

Outcome & impact — how I'd measure it

  • Acquisition conversion and first-device activation rate.
  • Time from sign-up to first monitored device.
  • Step-level drop-off across the acquisition path.
  • Retention and expansion from one device to many.

What I'd do next · Reflections

ThingSpace taught me to design for a huge range of sophistication in one product — the same screens serving a curious newcomer and a fleet manager with thousands of endpoints. That tension, and the barrier-to-entry problem, led directly into Digital First.
