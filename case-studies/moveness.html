Project overview

Moveness is a local movement platform piloting in the Northeast Houston corridor (Kingwood, Humble, Porter, New Caney). The primary surface is a mobile-web progressive web app, alongside a functional build. It is structured as a three-layer flywheel — content brings people in, a neighborhood directory is the substance, and actions (visits, day passes, check-ins) are the payoff — with the brand line Find your fit. Move well.

The problem

Finding the right place to train is a high-friction, low-trust decision. A ranked list or a map of gyms answers "what's near me" but not "who's actually moving here and would I belong." Generic directories end at a decision and never convert interest into action; social apps create belonging but don't resolve to a real place you can walk into. Moveness is the attempt to hold both — community that makes a directory trustworthy, and a directory that makes community actionable.

Users & research

The pilot serves people choosing a fitness home in a four-suburb corridor, plus the gyms, studios, and recovery businesses that want to be found. I grounded the supply side in real market and business analysis (the Northeast Houston fitness market, gym and recovery-center economics) and wrote a member research plan.

I'm honest in the PRD about what isn't done: the demand-side member research hasn't run, so the personas and targets are pre-research hypotheses. The largest untested assumption is whether ~25 people worth following live within a twenty-minute drive of each other and will post for free — and that belongs answered before the build, not after.

Strategy & approach

The strategy went through real, documented evolution across versions of the PRD — from a comparison marketplace, to a people-led feed, to a local directory made trustworthy by content. Two decisions anchor it:

  • The content-to-action contract. Content must resolve to an entity in the graph — a venue, activity, goal, challenge, crew, event, or creator — or it doesn't publish. The differentiator was never venue attachment; it was resolvability.
  • Seven pillars as product structure — Train, Fuel, Recover, Mind, Life, Journey, Community — each with a declared topic, supply source, and safety class, because health-adjacent content needs gating from day one.

Onboarding was cut from eleven steps to five in about ninety seconds, ending in a populated feed, with removed questions relocated to the moment each one actually changes something.

Design process

Product definition

I wrote the PRD through many revisions, logging every consequential decision with its rationale and its effect on the risk profile — the kind of trail that keeps a 0→1 product honest with itself. It's paired with user-journey documents and a wireframe set deliberately kept greyscale ("ten screens, greyscale on purpose") so the structure could be judged before the skin.

Brand

Moveness has a full brand foundation — name, voice, a lime-on-ink palette, an athletic geometric wordmark, app icons, and real-world applications — expressed in a brand system deck and a mobile UI moodboard.

Build

It isn't just a deck: there's a functional Next.js application alongside the static prototype, so the concept can be used, not only reviewed.

Validation & iteration

Iteration so far has been structural — each PRD version resolves open decisions and re-states what it does to the risk profile rather than quietly absorbing changes. Pre-launch The real validation — the member study and a live pilot — is the next phase, gated (by my own launch criteria) on content supply holding for four consecutive weeks before any public launch.

Outcome & impact — how I'd measure it

No live metrics yet, by design. The flywheel defines what to measure:

  • Attach-through — share of attachable posts whose action is tapped.
  • Action rate — step 2 → step 3 of the flywheel, where this category of product usually fails.
  • Confident decisions — people who choose a fitness home and act.
  • Supply health — weekly posts across the pillar mix, from venues and creators.

What I'd do next · Reflections

Run the demand-side research before building further — the discipline I'd demand of any team applies hardest to my own idea. Moveness is the project where I get to be fully accountable for the product thesis, not just the screens: every decision has a logged rationale and a stated risk, and the hardest ones (will people post for free, can a directory stay trustworthy as content widens, how to keep health content safe) are named rather than hidden. That's the standard I want to bring to any team.
