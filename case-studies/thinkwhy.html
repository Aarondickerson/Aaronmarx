Project overview

ThinkWhy delivers labor-market intelligence through a SaaS product — wages, demand, talent supply, and trends for an evolving workforce. I built a scalable design system to support a data-dense product that needed to expand without each new view being designed from scratch.

The problem

Data products fracture fast. As ThinkWhy added views and metrics, inconsistency in charts, tables, filters, and layout made the data harder to trust and slower to build. The design problem was twofold: make dense labor-market data legible and comparable, and give the team a system that keeps the product coherent as it scales.

Users & research

Primary users are workforce and HR decision-makers and analysts who need to interpret labor-market data and act on it. I designed against the need for legibility, comparability, and trust in a data-heavy product.

Strategy & approach

Treat the design system as the product's backbone: consistent data-visualization patterns, reusable components, and clear typographic hierarchy so numbers are easy to read and compare. Design the system to scale — new views should compose from existing parts, not invent new ones.

Design process

A component library and data-visualization patterns covering tables, charts, filters, and layout, documented for reuse across the product. Specific components and screens are available on request.

Validation & iteration

For a design system, the signals that it's working are adoption, consistency, and build velocity — the measures I'd track to validate it.

Outcome & impact — how I'd measure it

  • Component reuse / coverage across the product.
  • Design-to-build velocity for new views.
  • Visual and interaction consistency across modules.
  • Comprehension and trust in the data presentation.

What I'd do next · Reflections

ThinkWhy is where I learned how much leverage a design system creates — and how making dense data legible is a design problem, not a decoration one. That systems mindset carried straight into the multi-platform design system work at Match and the token-based component work at Cisco.
